To begin with, let's see \textit{where} we are playing now. Our infinite board should be able to have also infinite dimensions and every one of those could be or not infinite as well.

Let's begin with the simplest case (I hope), let's play on a two dimensional board that has one infinite dimension.

Let $G$ be a go board such that,
$$G=\{1,2,\dots,n\}\times \mathbb{Z}.$$
for $n\in \mathbb{N}$. We'll use $\mathbb{Z}$ for simplicity as it's a better (easier) representation of the infinite board.
As the number of players does not matter at all, let's use only two players and, the canonical adjacency.
Our game will be $\Gamma=(G,E,A_{ort},\Delta_{Go_{\infty}})$.
At least for now, we are not sure how $\Delta_{Go_{\infty}}$ works but, we will get there.

Our board could (and for us, will) look like this, Figure~\ref{first_inf_board}. Now, the very first change in this new game or extention of one its the next. A player in the infinite game of Go its allowed to play a hyperplane of stones. a hyperplane of stones might be or not infinite (that correspond to the player to choose), the only rule is that the hyperplane has to be o a lesser dimension than the board we are in. This is neccesary as if not, the difference between a really big finite board and a infinite one will reaming the same for almost the whole game. Being able to play infinite groups its a necesary change in the dynamic of the game. Of course, if a part of the hyperplane is in suicide, such play its illegal.

You might say, what about intersecting infinite planes? Very simple, as shown in the right board of Figure~\ref{first_inf_board}, a new line of stones that intersect another just splits, when the line touches a point that has a color that its not $0$, then the color that was just remanins, if the color its $0$ then it changes into whatever color the player might be. (I might need a proper definition of this but for now its more than enough.)

\section{Infinite SuperKo}

We cannot use Zobrist method for the infinite Go as we will need infinite many bits. We need a better method, let's try to make one.

If we remember the nature of the SuperKo rule, it's says that we cannot replicate a prior board via a stone play. Again, we are using this version of SuperKo (Positional).

Because of the rule, every game has to be a line, that is, as we cannot go back into the history of plays (at least it's a pass but will not take in consideration those). From the empty board to the very end, we have a straight forward path. Not even that but, because of the rule, the whole game of Go its a \textit{tree}. That is, starting from the empty board, there are 362 possbile plays (361 stone placements and a pass) and we cannot go back. From those, every posible outcome, that is, any end of a match can be found by following a line of unrepeated boards.

Let's define a way to measure how close are two given boards are.

First, lets notice that, in the very beggining, there are exactly one region and not much else in the board. That is, before the first turn, $|J|=1$ (the big empty region).

Now, the  very first turn will make so $|J|=1$ if the black player plays a stone or a finite line. If the player chooses to play an infinite one, then $|J|=2$. Lets pretend it just played an infinite group, with that, Zobrist method or any method that uses the stone itself go nuts and can't be use here but, $|J|$ it just a little bigger, finitely bigger. Now, this is not perfect as there are infinite many ways to play an infinite group so we end up with $|J|=2$. Before going forward, lets see an example.

for the $t$ turn, lets say that we have $p$ regions. Playing a line of stone has to be done on a region or regions if the line its too long, because of that, if the line played was along groups of the same color, we end up filling a region, barely or completely, therefore the number does not increase or could be lower. if we play on a region such that we don't fill it completely, we could do this by playing in the interior (We need a lemma for this), therefore spliting the region in two or, by playing on the side of the region. such play adds no regions if its not a capture and at most finite many regions if there are captures. finally, the total number of regions stills finite.
